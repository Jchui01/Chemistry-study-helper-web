Topic 1

Fundamentals of Chemistry & Planet Earth

  • Element: Pure substance that cannot be broken down into anything simpler by chemical methods (1 kind of atom, e.g., Na, He, O2, Br2). Water (H2O) and Ag2O are compounds, NOT elements.
  • Compound vs. Mixture: Compounds have fixed composition by mass, sharp m.p./b.p., entirely different properties from constituent elements, and are separated only by chemical methods. Mixtures have variable composition, melt/boil over a range, retain constituent properties, and can be separated physically.
  • Abundance (Memorize Order): Earth's crust by mass: O (50%) > Si (26%) > Al (7%) > Fe (4%). Atmosphere by volume: N2 (78%) > O2 (21%) > Ar (0.93%) > CO2 (0.03%).
  • States at r.t.p. (25 °C, 1 atm): Only 2 liquids (Hg, Br2), 11 gases (6 noble gases + H2, N2, O2, F2, Cl2); all others are solids.
  • Bunsen Burner: Always close air hole before lighting to prevent striking back. Open air hole → hotter, non-luminous blue flame.
⚠️ Hazard Labels: Explosive (NH4NO3), Flammable (H2, CH4, ethanol, Na/K), Oxidizing (conc. HNO3/H2SO4, KMnO4, K2Cr2O7), Toxic (Cl2, Br2, CO, NO2, SO2, Pb, Hg), Corrosive (conc. acids/alkalis), Irritant (dilute acids/alkalis).

Fractional Distillation of Liquefied Air: (1) Purify air (filter dust; remove CO2 and H2O vapour as solids). (2) Liquefy at −200 °C via repeated compression and rapid expansion. (3) Warm slowly in fractionating column: N2 (−196 °C) boils off first → Ar (−186 °C) → O2 (−183 °C).

  • Test for O2: Relights a glowing wooden splint. (Exam Trap: O2 is oxidizing/supports burning, NOT flammable; exhaled air is still mainly N2!)
  • Noble Gas Uses: He (balloons/airships: inert & lighter than air; H2 is explosive); Ne (red advertising signs); Ar (fills light bulbs to prevent tungsten filament oxidation).
  • Extracting Pure NaCl Crystals: Filter sea water → heat filtrate to obtain a saturated solution (dip glass rod to check for small crystals) → cool slowly at room temp → filter, wash with a little cold distilled water, dry on filter paper. (Evaporation to dryness leaves impure powder!)
  • Simple Distillation of Sea Water: Add anti-bumping granules (smooth boiling); cooling water enters condenser at the bottom and exits at the top; keep receiver open to prevent explosion.
  • Flame Tests: Na+ (Golden yellow), K+ (Lilac), Ca2+ (Brick-red), Cu2+ (Bluish green).
  • Chloride Test: Add excess dilute HNO3(aq) (removes CO32−) then AgNO3(aq) → white ppt AgCl(s).
  • Water Test: Dry CoCl2 paper turns blue → pink; white anhydrous CuSO4 turns white → blue.
  • Brine Electrolysis: Cathode (−) → H2(g) (fuel, NH3); Anode (+) → Cl2(g) (sterilizing water); Solution → NaOH(aq) (soap, waste treatment). NaOH + Cl2 → bleaching solution.

Key Ores: Haematite (Fe2O3), Bauxite (Al2O3), Galena (PbS), Zinc blende (ZnS), Cinnabar (HgS), Malachite (CuCO3), Quartz (SiO2 → Si).

The Lime Cycle: CaCO3(s) (limestone/chalk/marble) —heat→ CaO(s) (quicklime) + CO2(g); CaO(s) + H2O(l) → Ca(OH)2(s) (slaked lime); add excess H2O & filter → Ca(OH)2(aq) (limewater); bubble CO2 → CaCO3(s) (milky).

Ca(OH)2(aq) + CO2(g) → CaCO3(s) [milky] + H2O(l)
Excess CO2: CaCO3(s) + CO2(g) + H2O(l) → Ca(HCO3)2(aq) [turns clear]
Topic 2

The Microscopic World I

  • Subatomic Particles: Proton p+ (+1, mass 1, nucleus); Neutron n (0, mass 1, nucleus; 1H has 0 n); Electron e− (−1, mass 1/1840 ≈ 0, shells).
  • Isotopes: Same atomic number (p+), different mass number (n). Same chemical properties (same electronic arrangement), different physical properties (different mass).
  • R.A.M.: Weighted average isotopic mass on 12C = 12.00 scale: R.A.M. = Σ(abundance% × isotopic mass) (no unit).
  • Electronic Arrangement Trap: K (Z=19) is 2,8,8,1 (NOT 2,8,9); Ca (Z=20) is 2,8,8,2 (NOT 2,8,10).
  • Arranged by increasing atomic number (NOT R.A.M.). Hydrogen does not belong to any group.
  • Group I (Alkali metals): 1 outer e−; soft, tarnish in air, Li/Na/K float on water. Down group: m.p./b.p. ↓ (weaker metallic bond), reactivity ↑ (outer e− lost more easily).
  • Group II (Alkaline earth metals): 2 outer e−; harder & less reactive than Group I; reactivity ↑ down group.
  • Group VII (Halogens): 7 outer e−; F2 (pale yellow gas), Cl2 (yellowish-green gas), Br2 (dark red/brown liquid), I2 (black/purple solid). Down group: m.p./b.p. ↑ (larger molecular size → stronger v.d.w.), reactivity ↓.
  • Group 0 (Noble gases): Octet (8 e−) or duplet (He: 2 e−); unreactive, monatomic; density & b.p. ↑ down group.
  • Ionic Bond: Transfer of e− (metal → non-metal); non-directional electrostatic attraction. Exception: NH4Cl is an ionic compound with no metal (contains both covalent & ionic bonds).
  • Dative Covalent Bond (→): Both shared electrons donated by the same atom. Memorize: NH4+, H3O+, H3N→BF3, BF4−, CO, O3, HNO3, Al2Cl6.
  • Aqueous Ion Colours: Fe2+ / Ni2+ / Cr3+ (Green), Fe3+ (Yellow/Brown), Cu2+ (Blue), CrO42− (Yellow), Cr2O72− (Orange), MnO4− (Purple).
Structure Particles & Force Overcome m.p./b.p. & Conductivity
Giant MetallicCations + delocalized e− (strong metallic bond)High; conducts in (s) & (l); malleable & ductile
Giant IonicCations & anions (strong ionic bond)High; conducts in (l) & (aq) ONLY; brittle
Giant CovalentAtoms (Diamond, Graphite, Si, SiO2; strong covalent)Very high; non-conductor (except graphite)
Simple MolecularDiscrete molecules (weak van der Waals' forces)Low; non-conductor (unless ionizes in H2O)

🎯 HKDSE Boiling Point Framework (2M): State Structure + Bonding Strength + Exact Particles for BOTH substances (e.g., NaCl has giant ionic structure with strong ionic bonds between Na+ and Cl− ions, while CO2 has simple molecular structure with weak van der Waals' forces between CO2 molecules).

⚡ Conductivity Golden Rule: Requires delocalized electrons (metals s/l, graphite s) OR mobile ions (ionic l/aq, acids/alkalis aq).

Topic 3

Metals & Mole Calculations

  • K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al: Electrolysis of molten ore (expensive, needed for highly reactive metals).
  • Zn, Fe, Sn, Pb, Cu: Heat oxide with carbon / CO (e.g., 2CuO + C → 2Cu + CO2; Fe2O3 + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO2). 5** Trap: H2(g) CANNOT reduce ZnO!
  • Hg, Ag: Heat ore alone (2Ag2O → 4Ag + O2; 2HgO → 2Hg + O2). Pt, Au: Physical methods (found native).
  • With O2: ZnO (yellow hot, white cold), PbO (orange hot, yellow cold), Fe gives black Fe3O4, Cu gives black CuO, Hg gives red HgO; Ag, Pt, Au have no reaction.
  • With Water/Steam: Cold water (K, Na, Ca) → hydroxide + H2. Steam (Mg, Al, Zn, Fe) → oxide + H2.
  • Displacement: More reactive metal displaces less reactive metal from its aqueous/molten compound (exothermic): Mg(s) + CuSO4(aq) → MgSO4(aq) + Cu(s).

Requires BOTH O2 and H2O. Overall: 4Fe(s) + 3O2(g) + 2xH2O(l) → 2Fe2O3·xH2O(s).

Rust Indicator: K3Fe(CN)6 turns blue (Fe2+); phenolphthalein turns pink (OH−).

Prevention: Coating (paint, grease, galvanising w/ Zn [sacrificial protection even when scratched], tin-plating on food cans [rusts FASTER when scratched because Fe > Sn]), Sacrificial protection (Mg/Zn blocks; car body to battery − terminal), Alloying (stainless steel).

n = Particles ÷ 6.02×1023  |  n = Mass ÷ Molar Mass  |  % Yield = (Actual ÷ Theoretical) × 100%
  • CuO + Town Gas (CO + H2): Tilt tube downwards (prevent condensed H2O cracking hot glass); flush air before igniting (explosive); burn excess gas (CO toxic, H2 flammable); cool in gas stream (prevent Cu re-oxidation).
  • Combustion Analysis (CxHyOz): U-tube 1 (anhydrous CaCl2, absorbs H2O) MUST precede U-tube 2 (KOH, absorbs CO2 + H2O). Find O by subtraction: m(O) = sample − m(C) − m(H).
  • Limiting Reactant: Divide each reactant's moles by its equation coefficient; smaller value is limiting.
Topic 4

Acids and Alkalis (Parts 1, 2 & 3)

  • Role of Water: Acids (H-containing covalent compounds) must ionize in water to give H+(aq). HCl in methylbenzene exists as molecules (no conductivity, no reaction with Mg/CaCO3, but forms white ppt NH4Cl(s) with NH3(g)).
  • Basicity: Max H+ per acid molecule. CH3COOH = 1 (monobasic); H2SO4 / H2C2O4 = 2 (dibasic); H3PO4 / citric acid = 3 (tribasic).
  • Conc. Acids: Conc. HCl (corrosive: high [H+], non-oxidizing); Conc. HNO3 (oxidizing + high [H+], gives brown NO2); Conc. H2SO4 (dehydrating + oxidizing, gives SO2). Diluting conc. H2SO4: add acid slowly to large volume of water with stirring!
  • Drying Agents: Anhydrous CaCl2 (all gases except NH3); CaO (neutral & alkaline NH3); Conc. H2SO4 (neutral & acidic gases). Use inverted funnel for very soluble HCl, NH3, SO2 to prevent sucking back.
  • Soluble in Excess NaOH(aq) (ZPA): Zn(OH)2 → [Zn(OH)4]2−, Pb(OH)2 → [Pb(OH)4]2−, Al(OH)3 → [Al(OH)4]− (all colourless).
  • Soluble in Excess NH3(aq) (ZCA): Zn(OH)2 → [Zn(NH3)4]2+ (colourless), Cu(OH)2 → [Cu(NH3)4]2+ (deep blue), Ag2O → [Ag(NH3)2]+ (colourless).
  • Insoluble in BOTH: Mg(OH)2 (white), Fe(OH)2 (green → brown in air), Fe(OH)3 (brown). Ca2+ gives white ppt with NaOH(aq) but NO ppt with NH3(aq).
  • Base + Ammonium Salt (heat): Gives salt + H2O + NH3(g) (turns moist red litmus blue; collect by upward delivery or gas syringe).
  • Molarity & Dilution: M = mol ÷ V(dm3); Dilution: M1V1 = M2V2 (NEVER use when mixing two solute solutions!).
  • pH & Strength: pH = −log[H+]. Factors affecting acid pH: (1) Concentration, (2) Strength (complete vs. partial ionization), (3) Basicity.
  • Exothermic Neutralization: Heat released: SA+SB > SA+WB ≈ WA+SB > WA+WB. Temp rise ∝ (moles H2O formed ÷ total volume).
  • 3 Salt Prep Types: Type 1 (Insoluble) → Precipitation; Type 2 (Soluble non-Na/K/NH4) → Acid + EXCESS insoluble metal/oxide/hydroxide/carbonate + crystallize; Type 3 (Na+/K+/NH4+) → Titration + remix w/o indicator + crystallize.
  • Primary Standard: Pure, stable, non-hygroscopic, doesn't absorb CO2, water-soluble, large molar mass (e.g., anhydrous Na2CO3, (COOH)2·2H2O). NaOH(s) fails (absorbs H2O & CO2).
  • Rinsing Rules: Volumetric & Conical flasks → distilled water ONLY. Pipette & Burette → distilled water FIRST, THEN the solution to be transferred/filled.
  • Indicators: SA+SB (MO or PP); SA+WB (NH3, Na2CO3 → MO ONLY); WA+SB (CH3COOH → PP ONLY); WA+WB (none).
  • Back Titration: Add known excess X to unknown Y; titrate leftover X with Z. Reacted X = Total X − Leftover X.
